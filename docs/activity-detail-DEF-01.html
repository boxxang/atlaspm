Purpose

Turn what customers and the market are asking for into one ranked, signed requirement set that the whole program is measured against.

Everything downstream is an answer to this list — the PPA targets, the cost model, the feasibility call, the architecture itself. Until it is baselined and signed, every one of those answers is provisional. A requirement that arrives in week 30 costs roughly a hundred times what it costs here, and the difference is paid in silicon.

Inputs and working artifacts

Consumes

  • Customer RFI / RFQ responses
  • Field feedback and errata from the previous generation
  • Analyst forecasts and segment sizing
  • Competitor datasheets and public benchmarks
  • Sales pipeline and committed-volume view

Produces

  • Interview notes, per account
  • Requirement register — ID, owner, priority, acceptance measure
  • Conflict and deferral log
  • MoSCoW prioritisation record
  • Baseline sign-off sheet

Working artifacts are how the activity carries its own work. They are not the stage's key deliverables — how the two relate is below.

Relationship to the stage's key deliverables
  • DEF-D1 produces Product requirements document (PRD). The register is the PRD's body — this activity is where the deliverable's content comes from, not merely a contributor to it.
  • DEF-D2 feeds Target specification — PPA and KPI table. Each ranked requirement becomes a KPI line that DEF-03 turns into a number. An untraced target is a target nobody asked for.
  • DEF-D3 informs Product cost and margin model. Supplies the volume, segment and ASP assumptions the model is built on.
  • DEF-D4 feeds Feasibility report. DEF-07 tests this list against the candidate nodes — a feasibility study run against a different list proves nothing.
  • DEF-D6 gates Kickoff Go / No-Go decision record. The gate reads the sign-off sheet. An unsigned register is a No-Go condition, not a footnote.
Risks and watch-outs
  • Requirements not signed off by all stakeholders. Sales agreeing verbally is not a signature, and the Go / No-Go gate reads signatures.
  • Late customer additions after baseline. With no change-control channel agreed at sign-off, the register keeps moving and nothing downstream can freeze.
  • Requirements written as solutions. "Use HBM3E" is an answer; "819 GB/s sustained" is a requirement. Solutions here remove the architect's options in ARCH-02.
  • No acceptance measure. A requirement that cannot be tested resurfaces at qualification, when the only remaining fix is a respin or a waiver.
  • One anchor account dominating the list. Requirements that fit a single customer and no segment turn a product into a bespoke part carrying a product's cost structure.
