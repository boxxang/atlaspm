Why eight weeks is defensible here and would not be everywhere

Eight weeks is the volume-production number for a package that has been built before. A first build of a new 2.5D stack normally sits at the top of the 8 – 12 week band instead, because the process window is still being found. This template does not pay that penalty, because it finds the window somewhere else: PTV-06 puts the assembly through a process-window DOE in weeks 64 – 75, and PTV-12 freezes it at week 88 — eleven weeks before production assembly opens. Delete the package test vehicle programme and the assembly window has to grow to twelve.

The one thing this pass did surface

It is not inside the assembly window. It is the empty stretch in front of it — the time the interposer and the substrate have to be built in, which the published figures say must be complete at the moment silicon leaves the fab.

Twenty-six weeks clears a 16-week substrate with ten to spare and a 22-week interposer with four. It does not clear the 30-week substrate the same sources report from the 2022 – 2024 peak. That is a single-point buffer absorbing two independent supply chains, and it is the buffer that disappears first when advanced packaging is tight — which is the market these sources describe.

No date was changed for it in this pass, because nothing in the plan was provably wrong: the calendar was there. What was missing was a row — the template had no activity that fabricated the production interposer or substrate. PTV-05 built the test-vehicle one, and the production part simply appeared at ASSY-04. That row was added in the third pass below, and the margin it exposes is measured there.

What the re-review says

Every checkpoint is where the second pass left it. The programme is still 136 weeks; tapeout, first silicon, assembled units, bring-up and mass production did not move by a day. What changed is that 26 weeks of critical-path lead time is now work with dates on it, and the margin it was hiding can be read off:

ItemReadyNeededMargin
Interposer ASSY-10w96w101 · die attach5 weeksHolds
Substrate ASSY-11w92w102 · interposer attach10 weeksThin

At the top of its band — 22 weeks — the interposer still lands in week 98, three weeks clear of die attach. It holds under its own worst case.

The substrate does not. Sixteen weeks is already the top of the quiet-market band, and the same sources report 30 weeks when advanced packaging is tight and past 40 at the 2022 – 2024 peak. A 30-week build started in week 76 delivers in week 106, four weeks after the assembly that consumes it. Ten weeks of margin against an item that has historically stretched fourteen past nominal is not a margin.

No date was moved for this. Padding the plan against a peak-market lead time would be inventing slack, not scheduling; the schedule states the nominal case, and it now states it in a place a TPM can watch. What the addition bought is exactly that: PKGD-09 books the capacity in week 56, PKGD-11 releases tooling in week 76, and the two builds now report against dates instead of appearing at die attach. A new package material gate was recorded at week 76 alongside the MTO and wafer-out gates — package frozen and tooling released before the lots start, because they take longer than the die does.
